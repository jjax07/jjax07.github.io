About

I am a PhD student in History at the University of Saskatchewan, supervised by Dr. Jim Clifford. My research examines how settlement converted Indigenous land into private property on the Canadian prairies. Between 1880 and 1930, Saskatchewan developed an intricate settler network of homesteads, large-scale farms, and more than 900 hamlets, villages, towns, and cities. Combining close archival reading with historical GIS and text mining, my dissertation asks how this network functioned, who accumulated land beyond the homestead scale, and how its growth related to the pace and pattern of Indigenous dispossession.

I came to history from anthropology, completing an MA in Medical Anthropology in 2023. From 2021 to 2023, I was Co-Editor-in-Chief of the Pathways Graduate Journal in the Department of Anthropology. Drawing on the community-based methods I learned in anthropology, I joined a 2024 Traditional Land Use Study for the community of Sakitawak. Its mapping work caught my interest, and the training in historical GIS it required drew me toward digital history. Since 2025, I have worked on the Mapping Settler Colonialism project led by Drs. Jim Clifford and Cheryl Troupe, have supervised research assistants in the Historical GIS Lab, and write about how historians can use AI and large language models as research tools.
